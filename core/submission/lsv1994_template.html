Q2-1 / One-dimensional value strategies

Table I · Returns of decile portfolios

Stocks are independently sorted into ascending deciles of book-to-market (B/M), cash-flow-to-price (C/P), earnings-to-price (E/P), or weighted past sales growth (GS). For the three valuation ratios, decile 1 is glamour and decile 10 is value. The direction reverses for GS.

Timing, weighting, and size adjustment

April t formations use accounting observations dated in calendar year t−1 and April market equity. C/P and E/P sorts require positive ratios. GS uses five consecutive annual sales-growth ranks, normalized to percentiles and weighted 5,4,3,2,1 from most recent to oldest. SAS average-rank deciles keep ties together.

Returns are annual May–April buy-and-hold returns. Surviving original stocks receive equal weights at the beginning of each subsequent holding year; portfolios are not re-sorted. Months after a stock's final CRSP row in a holding year receive the corresponding size-decile return. Internal missing returns remain missing. Previous-December market equity determines each holding year's size benchmark.

Each horizon averages all available formation cohorts, giving 22, 21, 20, 19 and 18 cohorts for years 1–5. AR averages the five horizon means; CR5 compounds those means; SAAR averages the five size-adjusted horizon means. This CR5 is distinct from Table VI's mean of cohort-specific cumulative returns.

Q2-1 / Joint classifications

Table II · Two-dimensional value strategies

For each pair of signals, the eligible sample is independently split into bottom 30%, middle 40%, and top 30% groups on each signal. Their intersections give nine portfolios. The five pairs are C/P–GS, E/P–GS, B/M–GS, E/P–B/M, and B/M–C/P. Column labels give the first and second signal's group.

Independent sorts and portfolio construction

Both ranks use the pair's common eligible sample; C/P and E/P must be positive when used for classification. Groups collapse SAS deciles 1–3, 4–7, and 8–10, preserving the baseline tie convention. The sorts are independent rather than sequential. Annual holding returns, size adjustment, and horizon averaging follow Table I.

The calculated C/P–GS annual value-minus-glamour spread is $cp_ar_spread percentage points, compared with 10.7 percentage points in the published table. This contrasts high C/P and low GS with low C/P and high GS.

Q2-1 / Cross-sectional regressions

Table IV · Returns regressed on characteristics

Nine specifications are estimated separately for each of the 22 April formation years. The dependent variable is the stock's following one-year raw buy-and-hold return. The table reports the average annual coefficient, with its time-series t-statistic in parentheses.

Regressors and inference

SIZE is log April market equity in USD millions. E/P+ and C/P+ equal their positive ratios and zero otherwise; DE/P and DC/P indicate negative earnings and cash flow. Missing ratios remain missing. Negative-ratio firms are retained in the regressions when their required characteristics are available. The two univariate positive-part specifications have no negative dummy, matching the displayed models; the multivariate specifications include the displayed dummy.

Each annual regression uses complete observations for its own regressors, with an intercept and no winsorization. Standard errors equal the time-series standard deviation of the annual coefficient divided by the square root of 22. An all-zero negative-ratio dummy is unavailable for that annual fit; other slopes remain estimated. Each coefficient uses its own number of identified years for time-series inference. Other rank deficiencies cause an error.

Q2-1 / Extrapolation and fundamentals

Table V · Fundamentals, past growth, and future growth

Glamour and value portfolios are the B/M extremes (deciles 1 and 10), and the C/P–GS extremes (groups 1,3 and 3,1). Panel A reports valuation ratios and average size; Panel B reports past growth and the previous three-year stock return; Panel C reports subsequent accounting growth.

Accounting-growth implementation. Relative accounting year 0 is the preformation year t−1. For each annual transition, paired observations use a fixed ownership proportion equal to the inverse of market equity at the April starting the base relative year. Accounting amounts per dollar are averaged within portfolios and then across formation cohorts before growth rates are calculated. Geometric averages combine these annual transition growth rates. The paper does not fully specify missing-outcome handling or the exact price observation used in every transition; these conventions are stated so the numerical comparisons can be assessed.
Outcome availability and past returns

Fundamental ratios retain negative accounting amounts and are first averaged within each formation cohort. Growth uses the same firms on both sides of each annual transition; future accounting availability never affects formation membership. Market equity for later accounting outcomes is read from the separate extended legacy cache, through April 1993; accounts extend through 1993. These later observations do not extend the April 1990 holding-return cutoff.

Past performance is the equal-weighted mean of individual three-year buy-and-hold returns from May t−3 through April t, using complete 36-month stock histories. Missing returns are not filled. Undefined geometric growth from a nonpositive aggregate accounting level is displayed as a dash and retained with an explanation in the saved calculation.

Observed difference from the published sample. There are $undefined_growth_count undefined statistics in this Table V calculation because aggregate earnings or cash-flow levels are nonpositive. The baseline retains negative-book-equity firms: its B/M glamour portfolio has average B/M $bm_glamour_ratio, versus 0.225 in the paper. These cells are not numerical replications of the published growth statistics. No extra positivity filter or winsorization has been selected to force agreement.

Q2-1 / Consistency over time

Table VI · Year-by-year value-minus-glamour returns

Each row follows portfolios formed in that April for one, three or five complete years. Returns on each value and glamour leg are compounded separately before subtraction. Later formations are omitted only from horizons that cannot finish by April 1990; constituent stocks need not survive for the full horizon.

Portfolio definition and inference. C/P and B/M combine stocks from deciles 9/10 versus 1/2 with equal initial stock weights. The B/M choice follows the column heading and surrounding text on p. 1566; its caption instead says decile 10 versus 1. That alternative is saved as a sensitivity. C/P–GS uses groups 3,1 versus 1,3. Hansen–Hodrick standard errors use rectangular weights, lags 0/2/4 for one/three/five-year horizons, and an n/(n−1) correction. A nonpositive estimated long-run variance is left undefined rather than replaced with a different estimator. The stated HH estimator does not reproduce every published multi-year t-statistic when applied to the paper's rounded cohort returns; this discrepancy is retained in the inference audit. The published t-statistics below are transcribed, not reconstructed.

For C/P–GS, value outperforms glamour in $annual_wins of 22 one-year cohorts and $five_wins of $five_total complete five-year cohorts. The worst calculated one-year spread is $worst_annual percentage points. These are descriptive outcomes under the stated construction rules.

Q2-4 / Sample construction and replication limits

Filters and data-availability rules

  1. Formation universe. The original sample and Q2-3 use historical NYSE/AMEX exchange codes 1/2. Q2-2 uses NYSE code 1 or NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq codes 1/2/3. All cases require ordinary common share codes 10/11, and finite positive April market equity. Later exchange changes do not remove an original constituent's holding returns.
  2. Accounting and links. April t uses the last account dated in calendar year t−1, matched through active CCM LC/LU/LS links. Primary links and link quality resolve overlapping candidates. Book equity is CEQ. The current accounting and linking files are not point-in-time vintages.
  3. Signal availability. Positive C/P and E/P are required for their portfolio sorts, while B/M retains every finite ratio. GS requires five valid growth observations with consecutive years and positive sales levels. Double sorts require both signals.
  4. Holding returns. Missing internal monthly returns are not replaced with zero. Annual stock returns require twelve valid months after the post-disappearance replacement rule. Raw and size-adjusted results can use different numbers of stocks when December size is unavailable. No separate legacy delisting-return adjustment is added.
  5. Currency convention. The Q2-1 baseline has $currency_count formation observations with non-USD accounts divided by USD market equity, reproducing the supplied notebook's unconverted ratios. This currency inconsistency limits interpretation of those ratios. The selected restriction is $currency_setting. A USD-only sensitivity is available and stored separately.
  6. Table-specific availability. Table IV uses model-specific complete cases. Table V uses paired accounting outcomes and complete past-return histories without changing formation eligibility. Table VI requires complete calendar horizons; the original sample has 22/20/18 cohorts at one/three/five years. Extended-sample counts are reported in each comparison. Table V future growth uses only cohorts with complete five-year follow-up inside the applicable sample endpoint, without filtering portfolio formation by future firm survival.

Table I has an independently replayed reference program. The remaining tables implement the paper's stated definitions with the explicit conventions above, tested on constructed examples. Their comparisons show numerical agreement and disagreement with the published paper without treating a similar return pattern as proof of identical implementation.
