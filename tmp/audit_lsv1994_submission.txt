Q2-1 / One-dimensional value strategies
Table I · Returns of decile portfolios
Stocks are independently sorted into ascending deciles of book-to-market (B/M), cash-flow-to-price (C/P), earnings-to-price (E/P), or weighted past sales growth (GS).
For the three valuation ratios, decile 1 is glamour and decile 10 is value. The direction reverses for GS.
Timing, weighting, and size adjustment
April t formations use accounting observations dated in calendar year t−1 and April market equity. C/P and E/P sorts require positive ratios.
GS uses five consecutive annual sales-growth ranks, normalized to percentiles and weighted 5,4,3,2,1 from most recent to oldest. SAS average-rank deciles keep ties together.
Returns are annual May–April buy-and-hold returns. Surviving original stocks receive equal weights at the beginning of each subsequent holding year; portfolios are not re-sorted.
Months after a stock's final CRSP row in a holding year receive the corresponding size-decile return. Internal missing returns remain missing.
Previous-December market equity determines each holding year's size benchmark.
Each horizon averages all available formation cohorts, giving 22, 21, 20, 19 and 18 cohorts for years 1–5. AR averages the five horizon means; CR5 compounds those means;
SAAR averages the five size-adjusted horizon means. This CR5 is distinct from Table VI's mean of cohort-specific cumulative returns.

Q2-1 / Joint classifications
Table II · Two-dimensional value strategies
For each pair of signals, the eligible sample is independently split into bottom 30%, middle 40%, and top 30% groups on each signal. Their intersections give nine portfolios.
The five pairs are C/P–GS, E/P–GS, B/M–GS, E/P–B/M, and B/M–C/P. Column labels give the first and second signal's group.
Independent sorts and portfolio construction
Both ranks use the pair's common eligible sample; C/P and E/P must be positive when used for classification.
Groups collapse SAS deciles 1–3, 4–7, and 8–10, preserving the baseline tie convention. The sorts are independent rather than sequential.
Annual holding returns, size adjustment, and horizon averaging follow Table I.
The calculated C/P–GS annual value-minus-glamour spread is 11.25 percentage points, compared with 10.7 percentage points in the published table.
This contrasts high C/P and low GS with low C/P and high GS.

Glamour and value portfolios are the B/M extremes (deciles 1 and 10), and the C/P–GS extremes (groups 1,3 and 3,1).

Accounting-growth implementation. Relative accounting year 0 is the preformation year t−1.
For each annual transition, paired observations use a fixed ownership proportion equal to the inverse of market equity at the April starting the base relative year.
Accounting amounts per dollar are averaged within portfolios and then across formation cohorts before growth rates are calculated.
Geometric averages combine these annual transition growth rates. The paper does not fully specify missing-outcome handling or the exact price observation used in every transition;
these conventions are stated so the numerical comparisons can be assessed.
Outcome availability and past returns
Fundamental ratios retain negative accounting amounts and are first averaged within each formation cohort.
Growth uses the same firms on both sides of each annual transition; future accounting availability never affects formation membership.
Market equity for later accounting outcomes is read from the separate extended legacy cache, through April 1993; accounts extend through 1993.
These later observations do not extend the April 1990 holding-return cutoff.
Past performance is the equal-weighted mean of individual three-year buy-and-hold returns from May t−3 through April t, using complete 36-month stock histories.
Missing returns are not filled. Undefined geometric growth from a nonpositive aggregate accounting level is displayed as a dash and retained with an explanation in the saved calculation.

Q2-3 / Subsample comparison
Does the value effect weaken in the recent period?
The requested split is 1963–1989 versus 1990–2025. Both calculations use NYSE/AMEX common stocks, retaining the original paper universe; the actual formation and return dates are listed in the coverage table. Each subsample recomputes all five tables and Figure 2 using complete horizons entirely within its calendar window.
For C/P–GS, the average one-year premium changes from 9.31% (t = 3.41) to 9.09% (t = 2.17). The C/P tails premium changes from 8.34% to 7.09%, while B/M tails change from 7.22% to 8.91%. The change in average annual value premiums is mixed across the three Table VI definitions.
Table I AR, averaging annual holding years 1–5, has lower decile premiums for 4 of four signals (B/M: 10.49 to 9.24; C/P: 11.79 to 9.13; E/P: 8.41 to 5.35; GS: 4.78 to 2.75 percentage points). Size-adjusted SAAR premiums are lower for 3 of four signals. The evidence of weakening therefore depends on the return measure and portfolio definition; positive recent premiums remain visible in the tables.
These estimates do not establish a uniform disappearance of the value effect. The recent absolute t-statistics are lower for all three main annual strategies. A lower t-statistic can reflect higher return uncertainty; it is not a test that the average premium fell. No formal test of a structural break or difference in means is reported here, and the missing 2025 endpoint limits the assignment comparison.
Comparison spreads and differences are in percentage points. AR averages the five annual holding-horizon means; SAAR averages the corresponding size-adjusted means. Table VI three- and five-year spreads are cumulative rather than annualized. N counts complete formation cohorts.

Q2-4 / Sample construction and replication limits
Filters and data-availability rules
Formation universe. The original sample and Q2-3 use historical NYSE/AMEX exchange codes 1/2. Q2-2 uses NYSE code 1 or NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq codes 1/2/3. All cases require ordinary common share codes 10/11, and finite positive April market equity.
Later exchange changes do not remove an original constituent's holding returns.
Accounting and links. April t uses the last account dated in calendar year t−1, matched through active CCM LC/LU/LS links.
Primary links and link quality resolve overlapping candidates. Book equity is CEQ. The current accounting and linking files are not point-in-time vintages.
Signal availability. Positive C/P and E/P are required for their portfolio sorts, while B/M retains every finite ratio.
GS requires five valid growth observations with consecutive years and positive sales levels. Double sorts require both signals.
Holding returns. Missing internal monthly returns are not replaced with zero. Annual stock returns require twelve valid months after the post-disappearance replacement rule.
Raw and size-adjusted results can use different numbers of stocks when December size is unavailable. No separate legacy delisting-return adjustment is added.
Currency convention. The Q2-1 baseline has 403 formation observations with non-USD accounts divided by USD market equity, reproducing the supplied notebook's unconverted ratios.
This currency inconsistency limits interpretation of those ratios. The selected restriction is reference convention; no currency restriction. A USD-only sensitivity is available and stored separately.
Table-specific availability. Table IV uses model-specific complete cases. Table V uses paired accounting outcomes and complete past-return histories without changing formation eligibility.
Table VI requires complete calendar horizons; the original sample has 22/20/18 cohorts at one/three/five years. Extended-sample counts are reported in each comparison. Table V future growth uses only cohorts with complete five-year follow-up inside the applicable sample endpoint, without filtering portfolio formation by future firm survival.
Table I has an independently replayed reference program. The remaining tables implement the paper's stated definitions with the explicit conventions above, tested on constructed examples.
Their comparisons show numerical agreement and disagreement with the published paper without treating a similar return pattern as proof of identical implementation.
